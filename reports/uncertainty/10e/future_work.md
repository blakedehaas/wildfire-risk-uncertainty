# Remaining research questions, ranked

**Recommendation:** write the independent-study report now. The existing full-support five-study comparison already answers whether raw ensemble disagreement is a useful BP error-ranking signal relative to predicted BP. Any new claim that a learned or calibrated warning improves decisions needs a prospectively specified method and a **fresh geographic assessment set**; the current outer TEST outcomes have informed multiple exploratory choices. No option below was run in Phase 10E.

| Rank | Question and possible design | Data and independent evaluation required | Approximate cost | Potential to change the central conclusion |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Can calibrated BP reconstruction-error intervals support an actionable coverage decision?** Fit a simple error-quantile or conformal-style interval on geographically separate development predictions. Compare interval coverage, width, high-error capture, and selective MAE against intervals or rankings based on predicted BP alone; report each study/region. | Frozen model predictions and FSim BP on development geography; a newly designated untouched geographic assessment set or new spatial split. Preserve group separation and account for spatial dependence. | Moderate methodological and data work; no neural retraining required, but independent geography may require bounded frozen-model inference. | **High.** Could show that disagreement improves a calibrated decision even though raw variance ranks worse than BP; could also confirm its limited value. Coverage would be for FSim reconstruction, not fire outcomes. |
| **2** | **Does predictor-space unfamiliarity explain failures better than geographic distance?** Prespecify a TRAIN-only feature-space distance or support score, including missingness, and compare BP-conditioned error and disagreement across its bins. | Existing frozen transformed TRAIN features and outer predictions for exploration; fresh geographic assessment for any new warning rule. Feature scaling/correlation must be fixed from TRAIN only. | Moderate computation and design; nearest-neighbor approximations may be needed at this scale. | Medium. Could explain geographic inconsistency, but explanatory association alone would not overturn the finding that predicted BP beats raw disagreement. |
| **3** | **Can a lightweight learned error estimator beat both BP and disagreement?** Train on out-of-fold development residuals with predicted BP, disagreement, and a small prespecified feature set; compare against BP-only and variance-only models. | Truly out-of-fold development predictions and targets plus untouched geographic TEST. Existing Phase 9 outer TEST outcomes must not be recycled for tuning and confirmation. | Moderate to high data-engineering effort; model fit itself is cheap. | Medium to high for decision value, but it would evaluate a new estimator rather than rescue raw ensemble disagreement. |
| **4** | **Does a tree-based surrogate alter reconstruction or uncertainty conclusions?** Train a prespecified tree baseline with the same features, targets, masks, and geographic protocol; compare BP/FLP fidelity and a matched error-ranking signal. | Full training data, matching preprocessing and frozen geographic partitions, and a fresh evaluation plan if model choices are informed by current TEST findings. | High training and evaluation cost. | Medium for model-family generality, low for the established within-ensemble comparison. |

Rank 1 is the **single highest-value additional experiment if a decision-oriented claim is required**. It tests a practical trust output and explicitly compares it with the strong predicted-BP baseline. Its main prerequisite is a fresh geographic assessment population. Without that, the more rigorous next step is the final report, stating that uncertainty is **uncalibrated** and that the observed comparisons are descriptive. Predictor-space and learned-estimator work can inform a subsequent project; a tree baseline addresses model-family sensitivity rather than the present uncertainty question directly.

# Phase 10C — Buffer-zone inference and spatial reliability

## Question and result

Does proximity to eligible training cells improve FSim burn-probability (BP) reconstruction, and does five-member ensemble disagreement reflect any improvement? **There is no consistent five-study proximity effect.** Sampled DEAD_ZONE cells have lower BP MAE than sampled TEST cells in studies 1, 3, and 5, but higher MAE in studies 2 and 4. Mean between-member BP variance is lower in DEAD_ZONE in four studies, yet study 1 has substantially lower DEAD_ZONE error with *higher* disagreement, and study 2 has higher DEAD_ZONE error with *lower* disagreement. Within the buffer, error and disagreement also vary irregularly with distance. These are descriptive associations, not causal effects.

Disagreement remains positively associated with absolute error, but its error-ranking value does not reliably improve close to TRAIN. At 20% rejection, mean across-study top-decile error recall is 0.240 in DEAD_ZONE and 0.253 in TEST for disagreement, versus 0.577 and 0.576 for predicted BP. Phase 10C thus adds a near-TRAIN evaluation to Phase 10B's beyond-buffer analysis without rescuing disagreement as a dependable spatial reliability warning.

## Frozen data and method

- Five geographic outer studies use the frozen Phase 5B TRAIN / TEST / DEAD_ZONE memberships. DEAD_ZONE means outside the outer TEST set and ineligible for that study's TRAIN. It does **not** mean a new model trained without a buffer. A cell can be DEAD_ZONE in one study and TEST in another; each `(outer_study, cell_id)` is evaluated separately.
- The Phase 8 preprocessing record for each study has exactly the corresponding Phase 5B TRAIN row count; the cached inner-fold training traversal and Phase 5B eligibility flags agree for every cell. The five epoch-100 member checkpoints per study are loaded through Phase 9's provenance and SHA-256 checks. DEAD_ZONE and TEST samples have zero cached outer-training eligibility. Existing Phase 9 full-support TEST predictions supply the TEST comparator; no training or TEST inference was repeated.
- A deterministic, study-specific SplitMix64 hash of `cell_id` selects rows with hash modulo 100 equal to zero. This is an approximately 1% Bernoulli sample within **each** zone and study, independent of tile processing order. It selected 418,363 DEAD_ZONE cells and 1,109,771 TEST cells. Both zones have the same nominal cell inclusion probability, so within-study zone means are directly comparable as population means. Study-level results are presented separately; any across-study means weight the five studies equally. There was no outcome-based sample selection.
- For each study, a 270 m grid is marked with that study's **eligible TRAIN cells**, and Euclidean distance to the nearest marked cell is computed. For all 1,109,771 sampled TEST rows, the computed distance reproduces the frozen Phase 5B nearest-training distance **exactly** (maximum difference 0 m). DEAD_ZONE distance comes from this same transform. The Phase 5B 211.151 km exclusion radius separates TEST from eligible TRAIN, but it does not imply that every DEAD_ZONE cell has distance strictly below the radius: 1, 0, 21, 47, and 0 sampled DEAD_ZONE cells in studies 1–5 are at or beyond that distance. DEAD_ZONE is defined by the frozen membership flags, not by thresholding this distance.
- DEAD_ZONE inference uses the corresponding five frozen models, preprocessing state, Phase 9 inference function, and cached features. Exports retain cell ID, study, cached row, grid position, EPSG:5070 coordinates, BP target, mean BP prediction, between-member BP variance, six FLP targets and mean predictions, FLP validity mask, and nearest eligible TRAIN distance. FLP total variation is computed only where the frozen target mask is valid. BP variance is the population variance across five member BP predictions, as in Phase 9.
- BP MAE is `mean(abs(predicted BP − FSim BP))`; bias is `mean(predicted BP − FSim BP)`. Disagreement-error and distance associations are Spearman rank correlations. Fixed distance bins are 0–25, 25–50, 50–100, 100–150, 150–211.151, 211.151–300, 300–500, 500–750, 750–1000, and 1000–2000 km. Figures omit bins with fewer than 100 sampled cells; tables retain counts and values. Predicted BP strata, fixed before analysis, are `[0, .001)`, `[.001, .005)`, `[.005, .01)`, and `[.01, 1.000001)`. The common-mixture comparison averages zone-specific stratum means using the **same pooled DEAD_ZONE+TEST stratum proportions** within each study, over strata present in both zones. This is a descriptive risk-composition check, not adjustment for all geographic or predictor differences.
- Risk coverage rejects the highest variance or highest predicted BP within each study and zone at 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, and 50%. “High error” is the largest 10% of absolute BP errors **within that study and zone**, with deterministic tie handling from Phase 10A. Random rejection is its expectation. These fixed definitions avoid tuning on TEST outcomes.

The DEAD_ZONE to TEST MAE difference is −52%, +6%, −29%, +17%, and −35% in studies 1–5. After standardizing to the same predicted-BP stratum mixture, DEAD_ZONE MAE remains lower in studies 1, 3, and 5 (.003003/.006535, .001949/.002784, .001668/.002101), but higher in studies 2 and 4 (.006365/.005355, .002604/.002511). Thus the mixed result is not removed by this coarse predicted-risk control. Some upper BP strata have few cells; their exact counts and exclusions are in the tables.

Within DEAD_ZONE, the 0–25 km versus 150–211.151 km BP MAE is .002529/.003202 (study 1), .005794/.004621 (2), .002122/.001921 (3), .002945/.002613 (4), and .001330/.001327 (5). Study 1 shows the clearest near-TRAIN improvement, yet its disagreement is *higher* near TRAIN (1.404 versus 1.302 × 10⁻⁷). The analogous variance pairs are 2.130/2.334, .980/.949, 1.345/1.344, and .515/.440 × 10⁻⁷ for studies 2–5. The fixed `[.001,.005)` predicted-BP stratum still shows rising MAE with distance in study 1 (.002729 to .003480) while disagreement falls (1.558 to 1.427 × 10⁻⁷). Other studies have different or nonmonotonic patterns. [Distance by BP stratum](distance_bp_strata.csv) records all such diagnostics, including counts.

The disagreement–error association changes inconsistently across the buffer boundary: it is weaker in DEAD_ZONE for studies 1 and 4, stronger in DEAD_ZONE for studies 2, 3, and 5. At 20% rejection, disagreement's study-mean top-decile error recall is .240 in DEAD_ZONE and .253 in TEST; random expectation is .200. Predicted BP reaches .577 and .576, respectively. For example, study 1 disagreement recalls .187 of high-error DEAD_ZONE cells at 20% rejection, below random expectation, despite its positive overall rank correlation. [Risk coverage](risk_coverage.csv) gives all fractions, remaining MAE, recall, and error mass; the figure shows equal-study averages of normalized remaining MAE and recall. FLP valid-target mean total variation ranges .397–.475 in DEAD_ZONE and .405–.505 in TEST; this is a separate conditional FLP diagnostic, not evidence about BP disagreement.

## Costs, validation, and limits

The one-time sample-plan traversal took 15.4 s. Five TRAIN-grid builds took 36.7 s and exact distance transforms took 43.5 s total. The 8,192-cell pilot took 8.78 s for cold GPU inference; after warm-up, five full sampled DEAD_ZONE inference loops plus their writes totaled 4.20 s. These are measured components, not full end-to-end wall time. Peak work uses a single full-grid distance transform per study rather than full-resolution inference. No checkpoint, preprocessing state, split, buffer, or Phase 9–10B output was changed.

The sample is large but nearby cells are spatially dependent, the five study regions differ, and the same physical cell can occur in multiple outer studies. We therefore report study-level directions and magnitudes, with no pixel-independent p-values or causal claim. DEAD_ZONE versus TEST also changes region, target distribution, and predictor distribution; fixed predicted-BP strata cannot remove all those differences. Distance to a TRAIN grid cell is geographic proximity, not predictor-space similarity. Ensemble agreement is not calibrated confidence. The reference is FSim, so these findings concern **FSim reconstruction**, not calibration to real wildfire outcomes.

## Next step

The main scientific conclusion is that proximity does not consistently improve reconstruction or make disagreement a better warning signal. A further large inference run is not justified by these results. If uncertainty reliability remains a priority, use a *separately designated* development set to formulate and freeze any predictor-aware warning model, then assess it once on a fresh geographic holdout. Otherwise, carry forward predicted BP as the stronger descriptive error-ranking baseline and report disagreement only with its observed limitations.

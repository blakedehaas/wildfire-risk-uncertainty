## Algorithm and KL audit

The implementation matches [Chen et al. (2018), Algorithm 1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/chen18a/chen18a.pdf) in equal initialization, final shared-layer gradient norm, `G_i = w_i ||∇_W L_i||`, relative-rate normalization across two tasks, detached `Ḡ r_i^1.5` targets, L1 GradNorm objective, disjoint weight/network gradients, per-step Adam updates, and sum-to-two renormalization. Independently recomputing `L_grad` from all 1,864 continuation trace records agreed within `1.3e-7`, and every recorded post-step weight was positive and summed to two within `1e-6`. It uses the pre-update weights for both gradients. The code calls the network optimizer before the weight optimizer; with disjoint parameters and both gradients already computed, that ordering does not change this step's updates. The FP32 weight/gradient norm and FP64 KL calculations remained finite under BF16 forward passes.

The pinned [LibMTL GradNorm implementation, commit `4336804847eaa5e0b924b743d76beec7ac3fdc97`](https://github.com/median-research-group/LibMTL/blob/4336804847eaa5e0b924b743d76beec7ac3fdc97/LibMTL/weighting/GradNorm.py) likewise detaches targets and uses the two-task relative-rate formula, but starts adaptation in epoch 2, uses a first-epoch CE loss buffer, and parameterizes positive weights as `2·softmax(raw_weights)`. Our code follows the paper's first-step adaptation with direct weights, separate Adam at the paper's reported 0.025 weight learning rate, and a positive projection. These are documented implementation differences, not hidden arithmetic defects. Direct projection permits repeated contact with a tiny boundary; the trace shows this occurred, but the strongly asymmetric rate target already explains the direction of pressure.

The **material methodological deviation** is the rate numerator and reference: the paper and LibMTL use the same predictive task loss in both training and `L_i(t)/L_i(0)`, while our model updates use CE and its GradNorm rate uses unweighted minibatch KL. For fixed soft targets, CE and KL have identical predictive gradients, but `KL(t)/KL(0)` and `CE(t)/CE(0)` are different ratios. On the existing 16-step trace, step 2 BP's KL-derived relative rate was `0.643`; the CE-derived rate calculated offline on that **same trajectory** was `0.995`. At step 16, they were `0.474` and `0.723`. This is an arithmetic counterfactual only, not another training method run. The first two batches also differed sharply in target entropy: BP `0.0487` versus `0.0003`, FLP `1.1437` versus `0.3182` per valid row. Tile-based batch ordering makes a single first-batch reference sensitive to geographic target composition. Four folds began with FLP KL about `0.655`; fold 2 began at `1.447` and retained the largest BP weight after one epoch. This association is suggestive, not a causal test.

KL-based rates **amplify** the observed imbalance, especially at onset. They are not proven to be its sole cause: BP's CE also falls much faster than FLP's CE, and the published algorithm itself can suppress a fast-learning task. The paper reports a task weight below 0.1 in one of its own experiments; low weight alone is not an algorithm violation. Here, repeated floor contact and sub-1% BP shared-gradient contribution make the current KL/first-minibatch adaptation insufficiently justified for a study that values BP and FLP equally. One-epoch validation and training-only continuation cannot establish whether BP's later validation performance is acceptable.

## Resource safety

The 98 one-second samples include 71 with two workers active. Peak summed process RSS was `27.32 GiB`, but RSS double-counts shared mmap/cache pages. Minimum system `MemAvailable` was `24.89 GiB`; observed swap use stayed at zero. Peak whole-device VRAM was `2,733 MiB` on a `16,303 MiB` RTX 5080, including other resident GPU allocations. The WSL2 guest reports `30.2 GiB` RAM, `memory.max=max`, and no tighter container memory cap. Thus the **observed two-worker run was memory-safe**, although aggregate RSS is not a physical-memory measurement and host-level pressure beyond the guest cannot be inferred. The single-worker continuation peaked at `11.66 GiB` RSS and `218.49 MB` PyTorch allocated VRAM, with no allocation failure.

## Decision

**Hold production HPO.** The numerical implementation follows the selected GradNorm equations, with a documented KL-rate adaptation, and the two-worker execution passed this resource audit. The present calibration does **not** establish a defensible equal-importance BP/FLP training protocol: direct BP shared-layer influence becomes negligible, the weight reaches its boundary, and the rate definition is sensitive to the first geographic minibatch. Review the rate definition and initialization as a methodological decision against the paper and pinned reference, then require a bounded, per-task validation check of the chosen protocol before HPO. Do not infer predictive inferiority or superiority from this diagnostic, and do not change weighting or launch HPO without that review. The existing HPO validation scalar's CE-scale imbalance remains a separate unresolved gate.
